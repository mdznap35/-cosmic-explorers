# Educational content standards

## Science rules

- Use NASA and JPL primary educational/science references wherever available.
- Distinguish known facts from hypotheses and unknowns.
- Prefer simple wording without converting uncertainty into certainty.
- Explain when a visual scale or motion is compressed for clarity.
- Keep a source note for every topic module as it grows.
- Review planet order, orbital periods, units, names, and Arabic transliteration.

## Child-facing writing

- Short, concrete sentences.
- Introduce one new term at a time.
- Avoid unnecessary numbers in the first layer.
- Use an optional learn-more layer for deeper facts.
- Avoid fear, urgency, competition, or shame-based feedback.

## Initial facts requiring source review

The first planet catalog covers:
- Mercury: smallest major planet and closest to the Sun; very thin exosphere.
- Venus: hottest planetary surface, principally due to its dense greenhouse atmosphere.
- Earth: only world currently known to support life.
- Mars: red appearance associated with iron oxides; two small moons.
- Jupiter: largest planet; Great Red Spot is a giant storm.
- Saturn: rings are made of many icy and rocky particles.
- Uranus: extreme axial tilt, approximately 98 degrees.
- Neptune: farthest major planet from the Sun; very fast winds.
